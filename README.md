# Article I Found Interesting

[Unity says its AI tech will soon be able to 'prompt full casual games into existence'](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/unity-says-its-ai-tech-will-soon-be-able-to-prompt-full-casual-games-into-existence-),

# Why I Found It Interesting

As a game developer, I found this article particularly interesting, although also somewhat concerning. Unity’s plans to use generative AI to allow developers to create entire games through natural-language prompts show how quickly AI is lowering the barrier to entry for game development. While this could be a positive development by allowing people without extensive programming or technical experience to bring their ideas to life, it also raises concerns about the future of jobs in the game industry. If AI can handle more of the tasks traditionally performed by programmers, artists, designers, and other developers, studios may have less need to hire as many people. The article’s suggestion that AI could eventually lead to tens of millions of new people creating interactive entertainment is exciting from a creative perspective, but it could also lead to an overwhelming amount of content and make it harder for developers to stand out. I don't think AI will completely replace human developers, since creativity, game design, storytelling, and artistic direction still require human input, but it could significantly change the roles developers have and the skills they need. Overall, while I think making game development more accessible is exciting, I’m concerned about how these advancements could affect employment and opportunities within the industry.

**RYAN GAO**

It is interesting that Unity, as the game engine company, is talking about how AI could change the gaming industry. If developers can describe a game in natural language and have Unity generate much of the code and content, technical skills may become less of a barrier to experimentation. This could make it much easier to prototype ideas and allow smaller teams or individual creators to develop games that would have previously required much larger teams. Their acknowledgement from the engine's perspective that as the skill floor for creating games drops lower and lower, technical development is no longer the roadblock. This allows for the shift to creative design, opening up new possibilities for creators who didn't have the technical skill before. However, I also think this could create a new challenge: knowing how to evaluate, modify, and improve AI-generated work may become just as important as knowing how to create everything manually. Unity’s vision of bringing tens of millions of new creators into interactive entertainment is exciting, but it also raises questions about originality, quality, and how players will discover meaningful games among a much larger amount of content. (Which is already happening to steam with the wave of AI slop games) To me, the biggest impact of AI may not be eliminating game developers, but changing which skills are most valuable in game development, and how to express your creativity with it.
